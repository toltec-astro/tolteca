1. From measured IQ to the returned signal

1.1 Optical loading and readout power

DriveFit changes incident microwave tone power at approximately fixed optical loading. Skydip changes optical loading while holding tone power fixed. Together they measure the detector response over both axes. Loading changes the resonance frequency and loss; their local response is:

δxopt = Rx δPopt,   δr = Rr δPopt,   δr = β δxopt,   β = Rr/Rx.

This is a local relation for the optical component of the signal. Readout noise need not follow it. The present solver allows loss and frequency to vary independently; it does not impose a network-wide response ratio.

At fixed incident tone power, a change in Qr changes the nonlinear response. In the nonlinear resonator model, anl = 2Qr³Pg/(QcωrE*), where Pg is incident microwave power, Qc is coupling quality factor and E* is the nonlinear energy scale. Thus the power needed to retain a chosen nonlinearity scales as QcωrE*/Qr³. This explains why a drive setting selected at one loading need not remain suitable at another. Retaining a chosen nonlinearity is not by itself proof of optimal S/N. See Swenson et al. (2013), equation 12.

Combining this expression with b = 2anlr³ gives b = Pg/(2QcωrE*). Fixing b therefore assumes this combination stays constant over the integration; it does not infer microwave power from measured loss. Fixed hardware power alone does not guarantee constant coupling or nonlinear energy scale as loading changes.

The physical state can be written locally as r(t) = rref + RrΔPopt(t) + ηr(t) and xopt(t) = xref + RxΔPopt(t) + ηx(t). The remaining intrinsic fluctuations need not be independent; measured estimates also contain readout and calibration error. A slope in a short timestream does not isolate the optical component.

1.2 Effective detuning and optical detuning

Both detunings describe the same probe. The prime in x′ marks the effective detuning including the nonlinear shift; it does not mean a derivative. Their difference is continuous with nonlinearity, without an overdrive threshold. The probe frequency fp stays fixed during this timestream. The sweep fit supplies a reference resonance, loss, gain, coupling and nonlinear drive coefficient. The solver infers changing loss and resonance frequency from the measured IQ; it does not move the probe or refit the calibration at every sample.

For a singlet, let z be IQ after the recorded accumulation normalization. With the fitted complex gain H at the probe and complex coupling c, the actual calculation is:

w = c / [1 − z/H(fp)] = r + ix′
xopt = x′ − b/(r² + x′²).

What the IQ determines: with calibrated gain and coupling, a singlet gives two coordinates, r and x′. It cannot separately determine xopt and an additional freely varying b. The current solver supplies the missing relation by keeping the sweep-derived bcal fixed. This is a local model assumption, not a new measurement of power or nonlinearity. An independently measured loading law could test or replace that assumption.

Re-expressing the same solved loss as bcal/(2r³) adds no independent evidence, so that derived trace is omitted. In the exactly linear calibration, aref = bcal = 0 and x′ = xopt for changing r. At sufficiently low drive the nonlinear correction can remain negligible throughout an integration. No threshold is applied here to force fitted nonlinearity to zero. This network-0 comparison does not test the underdriven populations on networks 11 and 12.

For a multiplet, the responses overlap, so that one-channel expression cannot isolate a resonator. The joint solver finds each resonator's rj and fr,j from all participating probes. Its returned detuning is still xopt,kj = fp,k/fr,j − 1. Sweep anchoring and continuity choose an admissible trajectory; an unresolved sample remains flagged, rather than being silently assigned another branch.

The native output contains absolute r and xopt. The signal plots show changes about a median, explicitly labeled Δxopt or Δfr. Optical detuning names the model coordinate; it does not separate optical signals from other intrinsic frequency changes or give calibrated optical flux. Fixed b is a local calibration hypothesis: the earlier loading studies do not establish it over an arbitrary Skydip.

At a fixed calibrated state, δx_opt = Dδx′ + μδr. In coordinate order (r, x′), the transformation is J = [[1, 0], [μ, D]], so C_opt = J C′ Jᵀ. If Sᵣ = Sₓ′ and their cross-spectrum is zero, Sₓ_opt/Sᵣ = D² + μ². Equal noise in r and x_opt therefore requires a negligible nonlinear correction in this ideal readout-only case. A dark detector can also have intrinsic frequency noise or bath response, so darkness alone does not guarantee isotropic measured noise.

An active detector's optical response transforms as s′ = (Rᵣ, (Rₓ − μRᵣ)/D). Under an invertible local map, the full two-coordinate information sᵀC⁻¹s is unchanged. Comparing x′ and x_opt ASDs alone discards that signal-transfer information. For a dark detector both optical responses are approximately zero: optical β = Rᵣ/Rₓ is undefined, and the Skydip value must not be assigned to it.

3. What the additional amplitude-error estimate means

The previous working-zone plot shows a conditional calibration-error scenario, not a measured additional error in this reduction. Suppose the true local drive coefficient is b, but solving uses bcal = b(1 + ε), with correct gain and coupling. Write d = r² + x′². Then:

x̂opt − xopt = −εb/d,
D = ∂xopt/∂x′ = 1 + 2b x′/d²,   μ = ∂xopt/∂r = 2br/d²,
δx̂opt = [1 + ε(D−1)/D] δxopt + ε μ/D δr.

For a small intrinsic-frequency signal at fixed loss, the recovered signal amplitude is multiplied by T = 1 + ε(D−1)/D. The plotted bound is Eb = εmax|(D−1)/D|. With a correct calibration (ε = 0), this modeled bias is zero regardless of fitted nonlinearity. Being nonlinear does not itself imply an amplitude error.

For example, if D = 0.5 and b is overestimated by 10%, T = 0.9: the recovered small signal is 10% too small. Removing a baseline removes the constant offset but not this signal-transfer error. If a beammap calibrator has Tbeam = 0.975 and the science observation has Tscience = 0.9, their residual transfer is 0.9/0.975 = 0.923, about 7.7% low, assuming all other responsivity factors agree.

Along the optical response δr = β δxopt, the transfer is Topt = 1 + ε[(D−1) + μβ]/D. The fixed-loss plot below sets β = 0; it does not describe the complete loading response. More generally, δxopt = Dδx′ + μδr − δb/d. If loading also changes b, that term must be included. Gain and coupling uncertainties, their correlations, branch changes and large signals require more than this local singlet formula. Multiplets require the full joint response Jacobian. The current study has not measured a 5% or 10% uncertainty in b, so neither scenario is a numerical error bar on the final science signal.

Noise propagation is a different calculation

At fixed, correct calibration, δxopt = D δx′ + μ δr. With isotropic IQ readout noise and a single-resonator response, the readout ASD of returned xopt divided by that of x′ is √(D² + μ²). This changes coordinates and mixes loss noise into detuning; it is not an S/N ratio. A multiplicative change of coordinates alone scales signal and noise together. Correlated noise and optical loss response require the full covariance and signal-response calculation.

4. What should decide scientific usefulness?

Yes: the singlet/multiplet × nonlinearity matrix should ultimately report calibrated source S/N and contribution to mapping speed, alongside coverage and calibration bias. It should not replace them with counts of numerically valid components or an inverse sum of uncalibrated frequency-noise powers.

For a processed signal template s per unit source amplitude and noise covariance C, the matched-template result is S/N² = A² sᵀC⁻¹s. Compare matrix populations with the same source, exposure, beam and processing. Report each population alone and its marginal contribution when added to the combined population. Correlations between resonators in a joint group, common-mode noise and changes in cleaning prevent arbitrary addition of their separate S/N² values.

Only in the independent-detector, equivalent-response limit does mapping speed reduce to a sum of inverse NEFD². The current CSVs leave optical_snr and mapping_speed empty and record why. The earlier 149125/149101 study supplies a concrete evaluation pattern: propagate source templates through actual cleaning and estimate source uncertainty from sign-randomized maps. Its numbers and historical nonlinearity cuts are not results or selection rules for this 145609 comparison.

6. Prior studies, evidence and reproduction

The joint power/loading study found approximately linear scaling of b with microwave power over four supported power settings in 137224. Separate fixed-setting Skydip 142440 fits did not establish constant b across the entire loading range: fitted endpoint ratios ranged from 1.06 to 2.19 in five examples and changed with gain, fitting window and coupling treatment. These departures cannot all be assigned to detector physics.

The initial UID-562 loading study alone did not establish a network slope. The subsequent 112-trace network analysis provides the independent population evidence used here, including held-out forecasts and sensitivity to the readout model. It does not establish fixed b across every loading state, or identify those detectors with 145609 components.
