{{COMPARISON}}

For the {{MATCHED_COUNT}} APT identities suitable in both fit families, the median paired delay/affine frequency-ASD ratio is {{MATCHED_RATIO}}. This paired comparison holds detector identity fixed. Independent medians from different populations do not test which model is quieter.

The median trajectory slope increases with averaging time: 0.0163 to 0.0232 for affine, and 0.0179 to 0.0248 for delay, from 1 s to 8 s. This approaches the Skydip median of 0.0302, but seven 8-s windows are too few to establish a new optical response law. The table retains every slope, including the outliers.

At a fixed probe, x′ is the measured effective detuning and xopt is the returned intrinsic detuning. For the local fixed-b model, δxopt = D δx′ + μ δr. With an independently measured β = Rr/Rx, the x′ signal transfer is T = (1 − μβ)/D. Its signal-referred ASD is ASD(x′)/|T|. Thus a factor-of-two coordinate PSD or ASD change alone does not measure an optical S/N penalty.

The Skydip slope is converted from its reference frequency to the actual 145609 probe: βprobe = βSkydip fref/fprobe. The present qualified cohort contains no multiplets and no fitted a above 0.75. It therefore does not establish a multiplet sensitivity result or a benefit from heavy driving.

The two-coordinate estimate constrains its response to (δxopt, δr) = (1, β) to unity. We learn covariance weights on the first half and assess fluctuation power on the second half. Its reported gain is conditional on the Skydip direction transferring to this operating point. Per-detector slope errors and the observed trajectory disagreement remain in the downloadable table. This is not an absolute NEP, measured source S/N or mapping-speed determination.

The measured trajectory includes all fluctuations. Agreement with the Skydip slope supports the interpretation, while disagreement can reflect non-optical noise, model error or a response change. Neither result by itself separates those contributions. Dark candidates receive no β and no optical projection.

3. Evidence and selection

  1. Baseline: use the maintained 142440 Skydip analysis, its 112 isolated response-law contributors, the latest baseline assessment and the pinned APT roster. Reconcile source findings using exact acquisition/block/row receipts.
  2. Target association: use the shared Match1D full DTW correspondences against scans 2–8. Require at least five unique scans, no competing UID within 200 kHz, no duplicate target assignment and support in both Tune blocks. The 200 kHz window follows the existing observation-APT matching convention. Individual loading shifts make this a provisional correspondence, not a physical identity proof. Every alternative edge is retained.
  3. Finding-to-model binding: verify the stored plan's finding ID, initial frequency, initial loss and finding group against the saved candidate table. Do not transfer a model-local integer between acquisitions. Affine and delay candidates must agree before sharing these associations.
  4. Acquired readout: require no convergence, bound, rank, measured-feature-support or initialization warning; no bad contributing channel; a complete valid native trajectory; checked Python/native agreement; and residual RMS ≤5% of the measured complex sweep variation, centered separately within each probe. Fitted nonlinearity has no acceptance threshold. The whole joint group shares its calibration warnings.
  5. Measurement: scan 2 contains 7,802 samples at 122.0703125 Hz. Use Hann Welch segments of 1,024 samples, 512 overlap and constant detrending. The reported band is 5–20 Hz. No invalid sample is filled.
